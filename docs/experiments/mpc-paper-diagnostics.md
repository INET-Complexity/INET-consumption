# MPC paper diagnostics

The paired experiment applies the same positive nominal transfer to each household within a seed. Aggregate MPC is the sum of consumption changes divided by the sum of transfers. This equals a transfer-weighted average of household MPCs. Real outcomes use the corresponding real transfer denominator. Household means, medians, boxes, and negative shares describe the unweighted household distribution. Boxes show the interquartile range, whiskers extend to observations within 1.5 IQR, and outlier markers are hidden without dropping observations from means.

Independent simulation seeds define uncertainty clusters. The aggregate ratio standard error uses seed numerator residuals, with the finite-cluster correction G/(G−1). A single seed has no estimated standard error. This captures simulation variation, not parameter or empirical sampling uncertainty.

Impact summaries retain all eligible households. Cumulative summaries apply the saved `cumulative_mpc_identified` flag, where available. The raw and filtered samples receive separate reports. Income and wealth grouping variables are recorded before the transfer. Labour status uses the household reference person. The income label denotes the model's `income` field; component panels use employee income, employee plus transfer income, and rental plus financial income respectively. These are runtime concepts, not an assertion of equivalence to national-accounts disposable income.

`paper_mpc_summary_{raw,filtered}.csv` contains the total response and grouping results. Figure and summary groups share `prepare_paper_groups`: the `*_paper_group`, `tenure`, `labour`, `borrowing` and `constraint` identifiers refer to exactly the same samples and thresholds as the figure. Legacy `*_bin` columns are not substituted for these paper groups. Empty samples retain a readable CSV schema. `paper_mpc_cashflows_{raw,filtered}.csv` contains paired household-period diagnostic levels, with `_shock_tN` denoting treatment and `_tN` baseline. Housing cash need is purchase price less mortgage funding less net sale proceeds. Missing fields are omitted, not imputed as zero. New diagnostics require a fresh simulation; older saved windows cannot recover absent settlement states.

`paper_impact_mpc.pdf` and `.png` retain boxplots, horizontal labels, a black mean dot, and an orange transfer-weighted mean diamond. Housing wealth and illiquid financial asset panels show zero holders separately, followed by positive-holder quintiles. Their thresholds describe the displayed pooled household-seed sample. Household-type definitions remain deferred. The consumption-constraint panel requires a positive baseline goods-demand cut (using the regime floor-relative tolerance), within the ordinary liquidity-constrained regime. Arrears-only households without a goods-demand cut are excluded, along with support/floor-bound regimes. The comparison group is the baseline unconstrained regime. Panels built by the runner retain `baseline_consumption_cut_amount`; recent saved panels can use the equivalent captured impact-period baseline diagnostic. Panels lacking both must be rebuilt from their saved baseline windows rather than silently using the broader arrears regime.

Interpretation: these results are unrestricted model responses. Housing transactions, income, debt payments, consumption feasibility and goods allocation can change within the impact period. Their associations with negative MPC are descriptive and can overlap. A counterfactual fixing housing cashflows requires a separate accounting-consistent intervention and is not implemented by dropping households or adding post-treatment regression controls.

Paper outputs enable cash-flow capture automatically. Ordinary MPC runs keep that capture off to avoid large diagnostic CSVs; set `capture_diagnostics=True` explicitly when diagnostics are needed without paper reports. The notebook uses the same plotting function for display and export; household-type definitions remain deferred.
